## A path through system design

There is no single recipe for designing a system, but the questions come in a useful order. Take one user journey and go through these steps. You won't need every technique, and what you learn at the end often sends you back to the start.

1. **State the constraints.** Who uses it, how much load, how fast it must be, what failure or data loss is acceptable, what the team and budget are. The ground rules come from [HTTP](/web-platform/http/), [how a browser loads a page](/web-platform/page-loading/) and [browser boundaries](/security/browser-boundaries/).
2. **Decide who owns what.** Draw [application boundaries](/architecture/application-boundaries/) and [API contracts](/architecture/api-styles/), then decide where [client and server state](/architecture/state/) live, which [database type](/data/database-types/) fits, and which rules need [strong guarantees](/data/consistency/).
3. **Decide where and when work happens.** Choose a [rendering strategy](/architecture/rendering/) and [caches](/architecture/caching/). Add [background jobs](/architecture/background-jobs/) or [real-time updates](/architecture/real-time/) only where the journey needs them, and check what stale or delayed results users will see.
4. **Plan for growth and failure.** Find the bottleneck with [scaling](/architecture/scaling/), contain failures with [reliability patterns](/architecture/reliability/), and know how you would come back after a real loss with [disaster recovery](/delivery/disaster-recovery/).
5. **Get evidence and ship safely.** Use a [testing strategy](/architecture/testing/) before release, [CI/CD](/delivery/ci-cd/) and a [deployment model](/delivery/deployment-models/) to change the system safely, and [monitoring](/observability/logs-metrics-traces/) to see what really happens in production. Then go back to step 1 with what you learned.
